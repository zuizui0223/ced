# Ecological Modelling cover letter draft

Status: **editor-facing draft; author metadata must be completed before use**.

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“When full-world information misses the ecological target: Failure-diverse evidence for distinguishable futures,”** for publication as an Original Research / Research Paper in *Ecological Modelling*.

The paper addresses a modelling problem that arises when ecological monitoring yields a realized record without fully identifying the latent ecological system. Rather than treating full state identification as the reporting goal, we formalize which distinctions are required for a declared ecological target and when the observation architecture is reliable enough to support them.

The manuscript develops a finite modelling framework with five linked components: an experiment-induced compatible-world quotient; a unique coarsest target-safe refinement; explicit set-valued reporting when the target remains unresolved; failure-aware guarantees distinguishing repeated reads from independent observation modes; and a false-resolution, ambiguity and cost contract for adaptive monitoring and stopping. An exact finite benchmark shows that a measurement carrying more information about the full latent world can nevertheless be less useful for resolving the declared ecological target.

We believe the manuscript fits *Ecological Modelling* because its primary contribution is a mathematical and computational model of ecological evidence, monitoring and reportability. The framework is intended for model developers and users who need to distinguish state uncertainty, target-relevant uncertainty, observation failure and monitoring cost rather than collapse them into one generic measure of information.

The headline results require no external empirical dataset. They are verified through exact finite enumeration, deterministic replay and automated tests. The paper does not claim universal optimality of one experimental-design objective, does not equate nondetection with absence, and does not require full mechanism identification when the declared ecological target is already resolved.

The manuscript is independent of our companion work on structural identification geometry and mechanism-learning observation design. Those programmes retain their own methods and novelty; the present paper is specifically about **target-safe ecological reportability and failure-aware evidence**.
