\begin{abstract}
Ecological evidence can resolve a present state without resolving the future that matters for prediction or management. A population may be known to decline, an ecosystem to be degraded, or an invader to be present while several compatible latent worlds still imply different recovery, persistence, transition, or intervention outcomes. We develop a finite evidence framework organized around a declared prediction target. First, every experiment induces an exact quotient of latent worlds by their complete records; deterministic reporting is justified exactly when the target is constant on the compatible class, and otherwise the sharp conclusion is set-valued. Second, a unique coarsest target-safe quotient retains only distinctions required for present and future target reports. Third, observation failure architecture determines whether a nominally separating experiment can reliably realize that refinement: when mode availability is specified only by a lower bound, repeated observations inside shared failure domains face a non-removable worst-case guarantee ceiling. Fourth, adaptive policies are evaluated by correct deterministic reporting, false resolution, honest ambiguity, and expected cost rather than by full-world identification alone. In a 16-world exact benchmark, a target-irrelevant measurement supplies 2 bits of full-world information while a response experiment of accuracy 0.95 supplies 0.714 bits; full-world information gain therefore selects the irrelevant measurement, whereas target-safe design selects the experiment that resolves the declared prediction. A posterior-sample demonstration connects the finite theory to continuous Bayesian model output. The central conclusion is that ecological monitoring should be designed around distinctions among possible futures, not around resolution of the ecological state as a whole.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Ecological modelling often answers a state question more confidently than a prediction question. A manager may know that a population is declining but not whether survival improvement, recruitment enhancement, or connectivity restoration will reverse that decline. A lake may be classified as degraded while nutrient reduction and food-web manipulation remain observationally indistinguishable in their expected consequences. An invasive species may be detected without evidence that eradication, containment, or habitat modification will succeed. A system may appear close to a regime shift while competing models disagree about reversibility. Ecological forecasting has made the future itself an explicit scientific object, while also emphasizing that predictive uncertainty has multiple sources and should be confronted iteratively with new observations \cite{Dietze2017,DietzeEtAl2018}.

The gap considered here is not simply imperfect detection. Better observation can sharpen the current state while leaving the target prediction unresolved. Nor is full latent-world identification always necessary: two biologically different worlds may imply the same future for the declared target. Conversely, a small latent distinction can be indispensable if it reverses the predicted outcome. Even before observation error is considered, a finite passive record need not exhaust distinctions that could become observable under a later legal intervention. The relevant question is therefore not how completely the ecological system has been identified, but which distinctions among compatible worlds still separate possible futures.

This perspective also changes monitoring design. Generic information objectives can reward uncertainty reduction in target-irrelevant dimensions, and nominal replication can exaggerate evidence when observations share the same weather, access, observer, camera, laboratory, or other failure domain. Targeted and goal-oriented optimal-design methods already recognize that experimental effort may be better spent reducing uncertainty in a selected prediction or quantity of interest than in a full parameter vector \cite{VanlierEtAl2012,AttiaEtAl2018,ZhongEtAl2026}. The additional issue addressed here is reportability: what finite record distinctions are actually available, what target distinctions remain logically compatible, and whether the observation architecture supports a deterministic report within a declared error contract.

We develop that chain through four linked results. First, an experiment-induced ecological quotient gives the exact latent-world partition supplied by a declared record. Second, a target-safe minimal quotient removes distinctions irrelevant to the prediction while preserving every distinction required by declared future actions. Third, failure architecture determines which quotient refinements are trustworthy under imperfect observation. Fourth, adaptive monitoring chooses experiments and stopping decisions under an explicit false-resolution contract, allowing honest ambiguity when safe resolution is unavailable. None of the ingredients---imperfect-detection modelling, set-valued inference, state aggregation, target-oriented experimental design, or decision-theoretic monitoring---is claimed to be new in isolation. The contribution is their finite coupling around one reportability question: \emph{which ecological futures can the available evidence honestly distinguish?}

\section{Relationship to existing approaches}
\subsection{Occupancy and imperfect detection}
Occupancy models already separate latent occurrence from observation error; nondetection is not interpreted as absence when detection probability is below one \cite{MacKenzieEtAl2002}. Generalized occupancy models also accommodate false-positive as well as false-negative observations \cite{RoyleLink2006}. Paper B therefore does not propose an alternative estimator of occurrence. Occupancy or related latent-state models can instead supply posterior mass over candidate worlds. The additional question asked here is whether the worlds still supported after state estimation imply one target value or several, and which future experiment is required to separate target-relevant worlds.

\subsection{Partial identification and set-valued inference}
Partial-identification theory already treats a set of feasible values as the correct object when available data and maintained assumptions do not point-identify a quantity \cite{Manski2003}. Set-valued reporting is therefore not itself a novelty claim. Here the set is tied to a declared finite ecological experiment and then embedded in an action-stable target quotient: the framework records which target distinctions remain compatible, which can be removed without changing declared future reports, and whether another experiment can safely shrink the target set.

\subsection{Structural uncertainty, model averaging, and ecological prediction}
Ecology has a substantial literature on structural uncertainty, predictive inference, model averaging, and forecasting \cite{DormannEtAl2018,Dietze2017,DietzeEtAl2018}. These approaches can retain several plausible models and propagate their uncertainty into predictions. Target-safe modelling addresses a narrower abstraction question. It asks whether differences among the retained models are necessary for a particular target: biologically different worlds may be collapsed if their declared future reports and action successors agree, whereas a subtle difference must remain explicit if it reverses the target. The framework does not claim that model averaging cannot represent structural uncertainty; it characterizes which structural distinctions a declared report cannot safely ignore.

\subsection{State abstraction and model minimization}
State aggregation, behavioural equivalence, bisimulation, partition refinement, and minimal reduced models are established ideas in stochastic planning and control. In particular, bisimulation-based state equivalence can support reduced Markov decision processes that preserve policy-relevant behaviour \cite{GivanDeanGreig2003}. Paper B does not claim invention of equivalence classes or partition minimization. Its quotient is instead indexed by a declared ecological experiment and prediction target: it starts from what the record can distinguish, retains distinctions required for target reports under declared future actions, permits exact set-valued fallback when those reports are unresolved, and is subsequently constrained by observation-failure and false-resolution contracts.

\subsection{Adaptive monitoring, Bayesian design, targeted OED, and value of information}
Monitoring linked to explicit scientific or management objectives is well established \cite{NicholsWilliams2006}. Adaptive monitoring is explicitly question-driven and allows monitoring programs to change as information and questions evolve \cite{LindenmayerLikens2009}; adaptive management likewise couples learning and sequential resource decisions under uncertainty \cite{Williams2011}. Bayesian experimental design is more general still: experiments can be selected by a declared expected utility or loss \cite{ChalonerVerdinelli1995}. More specifically, targeted experiment design can selectively reduce uncertainty in a prediction of interest \cite{VanlierEtAl2012}, and goal-oriented optimal experimental design can optimize uncertainty in an experiment end-goal or predictive quantity of interest rather than in the full parameter itself \cite{AttiaEtAl2018}. Recent nonlinear GO-OED likewise maximizes expected information gain on predictive quantities of interest \cite{ZhongEtAl2026}. Ecological value-of-information analysis directly values additional data through their expected improvement in management outcomes \cite{CanessaEtAl2015}.

Target-safe design is therefore not offered as the first target-oriented experiment design, nor as proof that Bayesian design or VOI cannot target decisions. Its narrower object is a finite reportability interface: the record induces compatible worlds; a coarsest action-stable quotient preserves only distinctions required for the declared target; unresolved targets retain a sharp set-valued report; and observation failure plus a false-resolution contract determines whether a singleton can be defended. Targeted/goal-oriented OED and target-safe design may recommend the same follow-up experiment. The distinction lies in the explicit compatible-world, abstention, quotient, and failure-contract semantics rather than in target orientation itself.

\section{Four linked results}
A finite ecological evidence problem contains a latent-world set $W$, experiment family $\mathcal{D}$, observation contract, failure-factor architecture, action grammar, costs, and target map $T:W\to Y$.

\subsection{Result 1: experiment-induced quotient and honest reporting}
For design $D$, let $\operatorname{record}_D(w)$ denote the complete record generated under world $w$.

\begin{definition}[Experiment-induced quotient]
For a design $D$,
\[
w\sim_D w' \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \operatorname{record}_D(w)=\operatorname{record}_D(w').
\]
The quotient $W/{\sim_D}$ is the experiment-induced ecological quotient.
\end{definition}

An observed record $x$ induces the compatible class
\[
C_D(x)=\{w\in W:x\text{ is allowed under }w\text{ and }D\}.
\]
The exact prediction report is $T(C_D(x))=\{T(w):w\in C_D(x)\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Experiment information and honest target report]
Every report computed only from the experiment record is constant on experiment-induced classes. A deterministic record-based report of $T$ is justified exactly when $T$ is constant on the relevant compatible class. Otherwise the sharp exact report is the corresponding set of compatible target values.
\end{theorem}

The result separates evidence from interpretation: a record may identify the present state while leaving several target values compatible. A sharper point prediction would then add information that the experiment did not supply.

\subsection{Result 2: minimal resolution of future-relevant distinctions}
Full experiment records can preserve biological distinctions that do not alter the prediction. The appropriate abstraction is therefore target-relative.

\begin{theorem}[Unique coarsest target-safe quotient]
For a declared finite evidence problem, there exists a unique coarsest equivalence relation that preserves current observations, preserves target reports under every declared future action, and is stable under action successors. Every other observation-preserving, action-stable quotient supporting the same target conclusions refines it.
\end{theorem}

The ecological interpretation is deliberately asymmetric: full latent-world identification is unnecessary when remaining differences imply the same future, but even a subtle difference must be retained when it can change the target. Model simplification is therefore judged by future consequence rather than by latent similarity alone. The fixed-point construction is a target-relative use of established partition-refinement logic rather than a claim that state minimization itself is new \cite{GivanDeanGreig2003}.

\subsection{Result 3: failure architecture determines trustworthy refinement}
A nominal experiment can separate latent worlds in an ideal record model while failing to realize that split reliably in the field. Under one-sided imperfect detection, any finite all-negative record remains compatible with presence whenever sensitivity is below one, consistent with the observation-process distinction underlying occupancy modelling \cite{MacKenzieEtAl2002}. More importantly, replication cannot compensate for every form of failure dependence.

\begin{proposition}[Worst-case shared-mode guarantee ceiling]
Suppose $m$ declared observation modes are independent and each is operational with probability at least $a$, while all reads within a mode share that mode's failure. Across all systems satisfying this lower-bound contract, no joint-detection guarantee valid uniformly over the contract can exceed
\[
1-(1-a)^m
\]
using within-mode repetition alone. For positive sensitivity, the guaranteed lower bound approaches this value as within-mode repetition grows. If the true mode availabilities equal $a$, this is also the realized replication ceiling; if true availabilities are higher, realized detection may exceed it.
\end{proposition}

Thus ten reads collected inside one shared weather, access, sensor, observer, or laboratory domain are not evidentially equivalent to ten independent failure opportunities. The relevant resource is not raw replication alone but the architecture through which target-relevant class splits can actually be observed. The ceiling above is a contract-level statement about what can be guaranteed from an availability lower bound, not a universal upper bound on realized detection.

\subsection{Result 4: adaptive risk-limited target resolution}
Once a target-relevant distinction remains unresolved, a policy may either stop with ambiguity or choose a follow-up experiment. Terminal reports are evaluated by correctness, false deterministic resolution, ambiguity, and cost.

\begin{theorem}[Finite risk-limited adaptive design]
For a finite policy family and declared bounds on false resolution, ambiguity, and expected cost, all policies can be evaluated exactly. A least-cost feasible policy exists whenever the feasible set is nonempty; unsupported terminal records retain a set-valued report rather than forcing resolution.
\end{theorem}

The objective is therefore not shortest full identification. Monitoring can stop when the declared future is safely resolved even if target-irrelevant biological uncertainty remains, or retain a set-valued conclusion when no admissible experiment can resolve the target within the risk contract. This is compatible with the broader principle that monitoring derives value from the scientific or decision objective it serves \cite{NicholsWilliams2006,CanessaEtAl2015} and with the established idea that an experiment may be optimized for a downstream prediction rather than complete parameter resolution \cite{VanlierEtAl2012,AttiaEtAl2018}.

\section{Ecological scope}
The same structure appears across distinct ecological problems. Degraded ecosystems can have a well-resolved present condition but competing recovery mechanisms; invasive populations can be detected while eradication response remains unresolved; declining populations can share the same observed trend while differing in survival, recruitment, or dispersal limitation; and alternative-state models can agree on current regime membership while disagreeing about reversibility. In each case the relevant latent distinction is the one that changes the declared future. The framework complements ecological forecasting by asking a logically prior reportability question on the declared finite support: which alternative futures have actually been separated by the current evidence? \cite{Dietze2017}

\section{Software implementation}
The implementation accepts ordinary finite world and record objects. For example, a system can be within a management threshold or degraded, and degraded worlds can differ in recoverability:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
from ced.experiment_quotient import ExperimentInducedQuotient

worlds = ((0, 0), (0, 1), (1, 0), (1, 1))

def target(w):
    degraded, recoverable = w
    if not degraded:
        return "stable"
    return "recover" if recoverable else "persistent degradation"

state_screen = ExperimentInducedQuotient.from_functions(
    worlds,
    lambda w: "degraded" if w[0] else "within threshold",
    target,
)

print(state_screen.exact_report_for_record("degraded"))
# {'recover', 'persistent degradation'}
\end{lstlisting}

The state screen resolves degradation but not recovery. The software exports compatible worlds, target sets, quotient refinements, failure witnesses, and policy metrics as auditable JSON and CSV outputs.

\section{Comparative benchmark}
\subsection{Latent structure}
The benchmark contains 16 worlds crossing whether a management-relevant condition is absent or present, two possible response types, and a four-level latent attribute that is biologically observable but irrelevant to the declared prediction target. This nuisance dimension creates a distinction that is valuable for full-world identification but worthless for the focal target.

\subsection{Compared strategies}
All strategies begin from the same posterior after state screening and apply the same terminal false-resolution contract. \emph{State-only} stops after screening. \emph{Full identification} measures both response type and the nuisance attribute. \emph{Full-world expected information gain} selects the single experiment with the greatest exact mutual information about the 16-world latent state. \emph{Target-safe risk-limited} selects the least-cost experiment with positive probability of producing a contract-valid deterministic target report and otherwise stops with ambiguity.

\subsection{Experiment-choice contrast}
A perfect experiment on the four-level nuisance attribute yields
\[
\log_2 4=2
\]
bits of full-world information. With an equiprobable binary response and response-experiment accuracy $q=0.95$, exact posterior enumeration gives
\[
I(W;X_{\mathrm{response}})=1-H_2(0.95)=0.714
\]
bits. Full-world information gain therefore selects the nuisance experiment, whose target-resolution probability is zero, whereas target-safe design selects the response experiment, whose target-resolution probability is one in the detected-state contrast. This counterexample is intentionally directed at full-world entropy reduction, a permissible but non-unique design objective. It does not establish novelty over targeted or goal-oriented OED, which already optimizes experiments for predictions or quantities of interest rather than the full parameter/state \cite{VanlierEtAl2012,AttiaEtAl2018,ZhongEtAl2026}, and it is not a claim that optimally specified management VOI must choose the nuisance measurement \cite{CanessaEtAl2015}.

At state-detection sensitivity 0.95, response accuracy 0.99, three independent state observations, and no common failure, target-safe design produces a correct deterministic prediction with probability 0.9944, a wrong deterministic prediction with probability 0.00557, and no ambiguous terminal report. Its expected cost is 4.129. Under the same posterior and reporting rule, full-world information gain spends its follow-up on the nuisance attribute and produces a correct deterministic prediction with probability 0.4500, a wrong deterministic prediction with probability 0.000069, and an ambiguous report with probability 0.5499, at expected cost 2.479. Full identification matches the target-safe reporting probabilities but has higher expected cost, 4.679, because it additionally measures the target-irrelevant attribute.

The comparison therefore establishes a failure mode of one untargeted objective, not the general novelty of target-oriented design. Its role in Paper B is to expose why the subsequent compatible-world, quotient, abstention, failure-architecture, and risk-contract machinery is evaluated against the declared prediction rather than full latent entropy.

\subsection{Additional dimensions}
The complete grid varies state-observation sensitivity $\{0.60,0.80,0.95\}$, response accuracy $\{0.85,0.95,0.99\}$, common-failure probability $\{0,0.15,0.35\}$, and shared versus independent failure architectures. Primary metrics are correct deterministic reporting, wrong deterministic reporting, honest ambiguity, expected cost, and effort spent on target-irrelevant distinctions.

\section{Discussion}
The main ecological result is not merely that state estimation and prediction differ. Ecological forecasting and predictive-inference literatures already foreground uncertainty about future trajectories \cite{Dietze2017,DietzeEtAl2018,DormannEtAl2018}, and targeted/goal-oriented OED already recognizes that reducing full parameter uncertainty need not be the right objective for a downstream prediction \cite{VanlierEtAl2012,AttiaEtAl2018,ZhongEtAl2026}. The additional claim here is about \emph{finite reportability}: evidence for a declared prediction is represented by which target values remain compatible with the record, which action-stable latent distinctions are necessary to preserve those reports, and whether the observation architecture plus false-resolution contract supports a singleton rather than a set-valued conclusion.

This principle changes how model complexity is handled inside that reportability layer. Full mechanism identification is unnecessary when remaining distinctions cannot alter the declared future. Conversely, a small mechanistic difference must remain explicit when it reverses recovery, persistence, transition, or intervention response. The target-safe quotient formalizes this asymmetry and makes honest set-valued reporting the default when the evidence has not crossed it. This use of a coarsest action-stable partition is intentionally positioned next to, rather than against, established state-abstraction and bisimulation methods \cite{GivanDeanGreig2003}.

Observation architecture is part of that logic rather than a secondary implementation detail. A target-relevant experiment is useful only if its record can be trusted. With exact failure probabilities, shared failures can impose a realized resolution ceiling; with availability specified only by lower bounds, they instead impose a ceiling on the resolution guarantee that can be certified uniformly from the declared contract. In either case, additional within-mode replication cannot substitute for genuinely distinct failure opportunities when a strong guarantee is required. The framework therefore builds on, rather than replaces, the observation-process logic of imperfect-detection models \cite{MacKenzieEtAl2002,RoyleLink2006}.

The framework remains conditional on the declared world set, actions, observation models, targets, costs, and finite support. It does not make model misspecification disappear, infer failure factors from data, or replace management utility when utilities are available and defensible. Bayesian design, targeted/goal-oriented OED, and ecological VOI can encode richer utilities, quantities of interest, and decision consequences \cite{ChalonerVerdinelli1995,VanlierEtAl2012,AttiaEtAl2018,CanessaEtAl2015}; target-safe reporting instead exposes an explicit compatible-world, abstention, quotient, failure, and error contract. Its value is auditability: every deterministic conclusion can be traced to the target-relevant worlds excluded by the evidence, and every retained ambiguity to a future distinction that remains unresolved.

The practical shift is therefore not simply from full-state to target-oriented experimental design; that shift already exists in adjacent optimal-design work. It is from treating target uncertainty only as an optimization objective to also treating it as a finite reporting object with a sharp abstention state and an auditable observation contract. This is consistent with the longstanding principle that monitoring should be driven by explicit scientific or management objectives \cite{NicholsWilliams2006,LindenmayerLikens2009,Williams2011}. Monitoring should spend effort on the latent distinctions that change the declared future, diversify the observation architecture needed to reveal them, and stop once the target is defensibly resolved.

\section{Methods and reproducibility}
The quotient is constructed by grouping worlds with identical records. In the stochastic benchmark, state screening, response typing, and nuisance measurement are separate likelihood kernels. For a belief distribution $p(w)$ and experiment $e$ with outcome $x$, full-world information gain is computed exactly as
\[
I(W;X_e)=H(W)-\sum_x p(x\mid e)H(W\mid x,e).
\]
Every strategy receives the same posterior after screening and uses the same terminal rule: a deterministic target is reported only when its posterior error probability is at most the declared false-resolution limit; otherwise the report remains set-valued. Policy evaluation enumerates latent worlds, screening records, selected experiment outcomes, posterior updates, terminal reports, and accumulated cost. Shared-failure and operational all-negative paths that produce the same record are aggregated before posterior updating. Detailed detection frontiers, calibration bounds, heterogeneous thresholds, concentration bounds, and adaptive risk-spending formulas are supporting Methods or Supplement material rather than separate headline results. The reproducibility workflow executes the complete test suite on Python 3.10--3.12 and regenerates benchmark, robustness, posterior-bridge, figure, table, and manuscript artifacts.

\section{Conclusion}
More ecological information does not necessarily produce more defensible ecological prediction. What matters is not merely whether an experiment is target-oriented, but whether its finite record excludes the worlds that imply different target values and whether the observation architecture supports that exclusion within the declared error contract. Full latent-state identification can therefore be unnecessary, while high untargeted information gain can still be insufficient. A target-safe monitoring strategy resolves only future-relevant distinctions, retains a set-valued report when they remain unresolved, and attaches an explicit risk contract to every deterministic conclusion. The resulting design principle is: \emph{represent what the evidence can report about the future, not only what an experimental objective hopes to learn.}
