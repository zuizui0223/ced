\begin{abstract}
\begin{enumerate}
\item More latent-world information need not resolve the ecological future being asked about, and repeated observations need not add trustworthy evidence when they share a failure domain. We ask what a realized finite ecological record can honestly report about a declared prediction target.
\item A 16-world exact benchmark shows that full-world entropy reduction can select a perfectly measured but target-irrelevant attribute while leaving the focal prediction unresolved. Under equal effort, repeated reads inside one shared failure mode and reads distributed across independent modes support different worst-case guarantees. These are target- and failure-contract results, not claims that information or replication is generically unhelpful.
\item We formalize the diagnosis through \emph{finite reportability}: an experiment-induced compatible-world quotient, the unique coarsest action-stable target-safe quotient, failure-aware guarantees for trustworthy refinement, and an adaptive false-resolution reporting contract. The quotient results supply the formal reporting interface; the decisive applications are failure-diverse evidence allocation and target-safe experiment choice.
\item All headline results are verified by finite enumeration, deterministic replay, and a formal Supplement. The method complements occupancy models, goal-oriented experimental design, value of information, prediction sets, and state abstraction. It reports a singleton only when the realized evidence and declared failure/risk contract support it; otherwise the honest ecological prediction remains a set.
\end{enumerate}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Two monitoring programmes can collect the same number of observations yet support different ecological conclusions. Repeats taken under one shared weather, access, sensor, observer, or laboratory failure mode cannot generally substitute for observations distributed across independent failure opportunities. Conversely, an experiment can remove more uncertainty about the latent world while resolving none of the uncertainty in the ecological future being asked about. Raw replicate count and full-world information are therefore incomplete measures of evidential value.

The practical problem appears whenever a present classification is sharper than the associated prediction. A manager may know that a population is declining but not whether survival improvement, recruitment enhancement, or connectivity restoration will reverse that decline. A lake may be classified as degraded while competing mechanisms imply different recovery under intervention. An invasive species may be detected without evidence that eradication will succeed. Ecological forecasting has made future trajectories an explicit scientific object and emphasizes iterative confrontation of predictive uncertainty with new observations \cite{Dietze2017,DietzeEtAl2018}.

The gap is not simply imperfect detection. Better observation can sharpen the current state while leaving several future target values compatible. Nor is full latent-world identification always necessary: biologically different worlds can imply the same declared future, while a subtle difference can be indispensable if it reverses that future. The operational question is therefore not how completely the ecological system has been identified, but what the realized evidence is sufficient to report.

Targeted and goal-oriented optimal-design methods already recognize that experimental effort may be better spent reducing uncertainty in a selected prediction or quantity of interest than in a full parameter vector \cite{VanlierEtAl2012,AttiaEtAl2018,ZhongEtAl2026}. Paper B addresses the complementary reporting problem after a finite record is realized: which worlds remain compatible, which of their differences can change the target under declared actions, and whether the observation architecture supports a singleton report within a stated error contract.

We answer those questions through four linked results but present them in consequence-first order. Failure architecture first determines whether nominally repeated evidence can support a target-relevant split. Adaptive monitoring then exposes a concrete failure of full-world entropy reduction and chooses experiments and stopping decisions under an explicit false-resolution contract. The experiment-induced quotient and unique coarsest target-safe quotient subsequently provide the formal reporting structure that makes those conclusions exact. Their logical dependency remains quotient first; their manuscript role is infrastructure for the two operational results. The contribution is the coupling of these objects into one finite reportability interface, not the invention of imperfect-detection modelling, set-valued inference, state aggregation, or target-oriented experimental design in isolation.

\subsection{Relationship to existing approaches}
Occupancy models already separate latent occurrence from observation error, so nondetection need not imply absence \cite{MacKenzieEtAl2002}; generalized models also accommodate false-positive observations \cite{RoyleLink2006}. Partial-identification theory treats a feasible set as the appropriate inferential object when point identification fails \cite{Manski2003}, and risk-controlling prediction sets provide set-valued predictions under user-specified finite-sample risk control \cite{BatesEtAl2021}. Ecology has extensive methods for structural uncertainty, model averaging, predictive inference, and forecasting \cite{DormannEtAl2018,Dietze2017,DietzeEtAl2018}. State aggregation, behavioural equivalence, bisimulation, partition refinement, and minimal reduced models are established in stochastic planning and control \cite{GivanDeanGreig2003}. Question-driven monitoring and adaptive management are also well established \cite{NicholsWilliams2006,LindenmayerLikens2009,Williams2011}.

Bayesian experimental design can optimize arbitrary expected utilities or losses \cite{ChalonerVerdinelli1995}; targeted and goal-oriented designs explicitly optimize selected predictions or quantities of interest \cite{VanlierEtAl2012,AttiaEtAl2018,ZhongEtAl2026}; and ecological value of information values new data through expected management benefit \cite{CanessaEtAl2015}. Target-safe design is compatible with these broader traditions and may choose the same experiment. Its distinct object is the finite reporting interface linking compatible worlds, target-preserving abstraction, observation failure, abstention, and a false-resolution contract.

\section{Materials and Methods}
\subsection{Finite evidence problem}
A finite ecological evidence problem contains a latent-world set $W$, experiment family $\mathcal{D}$, observation contract, failure-factor architecture, action grammar, experiment costs, and target map $T:W\to Y$. For deterministic designs, complete experiment records partition $W$. For stochastic designs, the logical compatible set for realized record $x$ consists of worlds assigning positive likelihood to $x$; posterior probabilities are then used only by the separate risk-limited singleton-reporting rule.

A target-safe quotient begins from the distinctions supplied by the current record and repeatedly refines worlds when their target values differ or when a declared action sends them to different current blocks. Because the world set is finite, refinement reaches a fixed point. Full proofs of existence, uniqueness, minimality, finite-action-word preservation, the failure guarantee, and finite policy existence are given in the Supplement.

\subsection{Observation-failure contract}
One-sided detection separates per-read sensitivity from shared mode availability. Reads inside an operating mode may be repeated, but all reads in that mode share the same mode-level failure. When each of $m$ independent modes is operational with probability at least $a$, the analysis evaluates guarantees uniformly over the declared lower-bound contract rather than treating $a$ as an exact realized availability. Exact overlapping-factor models are evaluated separately when exact factor failure probabilities are declared.

\subsection{Workflow and software implementation}
The operational workflow is: declare worlds and target; declare observations, actions, and failure factors; construct compatible target classes; compare feasible experiment/stopping policies by error, ambiguity, and cost; then either report or experiment again.

The implementation accepts ordinary finite world and record objects. For example, a state screen may identify degradation while leaving recovery unresolved:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
from ced.experiment_quotient import ExperimentInducedQuotient

worlds = ((0, 0), (0, 1), (1, 0), (1, 1))

def target(w):
    degraded, recoverable = w
    if not degraded:
        return "stable"
    return "recover" if recoverable else "persistent degradation"

state_screen = ExperimentInducedQuotient.from_functions(
    worlds,
    lambda w: "degraded" if w[0] else "within threshold",
    target,
)

print(state_screen.set_valued_report_for_record("degraded"))
# {'recover', 'persistent degradation'}
\end{lstlisting}

The software exports compatible worlds, target sets, quotient refinements, failure witnesses, policy metrics, and deterministic JSON/CSV replay artifacts.

\subsection{Comparative benchmark design}
The exact benchmark contains 16 worlds crossing a management-relevant state, two possible response types, and a four-level latent attribute that is biologically observable but irrelevant to the focal target. All strategies receive the same posterior after state screening and use the same terminal false-resolution rule. \emph{State-only} stops after screening. \emph{Full identification} measures response type and nuisance attribute. \emph{Full-world expected information gain} selects the single experiment with greatest exact mutual information about the 16-world state. \emph{Target-safe risk-limited} selects the least-cost follow-up with positive probability of producing a contract-valid deterministic target report and otherwise stops with ambiguity.

For belief distribution $p(w)$ and experiment $e$ with outcome $x$, full-world information gain is computed exactly as
\[
I(W;X_e)=H(W)-\sum_x p(x\mid e)H(W\mid x,e).
\]
Policy evaluation enumerates latent worlds, screening records, selected experiment outcomes, posterior updates, terminal reports, and accumulated cost. Shared-failure and operational all-negative paths producing the same record are aggregated before posterior updating.

\subsection{Sensitivity analyses and posterior-support bridge}
The benchmark grid varies state-observation sensitivity $\{0.60,0.80,0.95\}$, response accuracy $\{0.85,0.95,0.99\}$, common-failure probability $\{0,0.15,0.35\}$, and shared versus independent failure architectures. Additional reviewer-facing checks switch the declared target, vary the false-resolution limit, and treat deterministic posterior draws from a continuous invasion-control demonstration as finite worlds. The latter is a methodological bridge rather than empirical validation; conclusions remain conditional on the chosen finite support.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
The complete automated test suite runs on Python 3.10--3.12. Deterministic scripts regenerate the benchmark, reviewer robustness analyses, posterior-support demonstration, explanatory figures, tables, and manuscript artifacts. Detailed calibration, threshold, concentration, overlapping-failure, and adaptive risk-spending derivations are retained as supporting mathematics rather than separate headline results.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Result 3: failure architecture determines trustworthy refinement}
A nominal experiment can separate latent worlds in an ideal record model while failing to realize that split reliably in the field. Under one-sided imperfect detection, any finite all-negative record remains compatible with presence whenever sensitivity is below one. More importantly, replication cannot compensate for every form of failure dependence.

\begin{proposition}[Worst-case shared-mode guarantee ceiling]
Suppose $m$ declared observation modes are independent and each is operational with probability at least $a$, while all reads within a mode share that mode's failure. Across all systems satisfying this lower-bound contract, no joint-detection guarantee valid uniformly over the contract can exceed
\[
1-(1-a)^m
\]
using within-mode repetition alone. For positive sensitivity, the guaranteed lower bound approaches this value as within-mode repetition grows. If true mode availabilities equal $a$, this is also the realized replication ceiling; if true availabilities are higher, realized detection may exceed it.
\end{proposition}

Thus repeated reads inside one shared weather, access, sensor, observer, or laboratory domain are not evidentially equivalent to independent failure opportunities. The relevant resource is the architecture through which target-relevant class splits can actually be observed.

\subsection{Result 4: adaptive risk-limited target resolution}
Once a target-relevant distinction remains unresolved, a policy may either stop with ambiguity or choose a follow-up experiment. Terminal reports are evaluated by correctness, false deterministic resolution, ambiguity, and cost.

\begin{theorem}[Finite risk-limited adaptive design]
For a finite policy family and declared bounds on false resolution, ambiguity, and expected cost, all policies can be evaluated exactly. A least-cost feasible policy exists whenever the feasible set is nonempty; unsupported terminal records retain a set-valued report rather than forcing resolution.
\end{theorem}

The objective is not shortest full identification. Monitoring stops when the declared future is defensibly resolved under the reporting contract, even if target-irrelevant biological uncertainty remains; otherwise the terminal report remains set-valued.

\subsection{Comparative benchmark}
A perfect experiment on the four-level nuisance attribute yields
\[
\log_2 4=2
\]
bits of full-world information. With an equiprobable binary response and response-experiment accuracy $q=0.95$, exact posterior enumeration gives
\[
I(W;X_{\mathrm{response}})=1-H_2(0.95)=0.714
\]
bits. Full-world information gain therefore selects the nuisance experiment, whose target-resolution probability is zero, whereas target-safe design selects the response experiment, whose target-resolution probability is one in the detected-state contrast. This is a failure witness for full-world entropy reduction only; targeted/goal-oriented OED and management VOI can already optimize prediction- or decision-relevant information \cite{VanlierEtAl2012,AttiaEtAl2018,ZhongEtAl2026,CanessaEtAl2015}.

At state-detection sensitivity 0.95, response accuracy 0.99, three independent state observations, and no common failure, target-safe design produces a correct deterministic prediction with probability 0.9944, a wrong deterministic prediction with probability 0.00557, and no ambiguous terminal report. Its expected cost is 4.129. Under the same posterior and reporting rule, full-world information gain spends its follow-up on the nuisance attribute and produces a correct deterministic prediction with probability 0.4500, a wrong deterministic prediction with probability 0.000069, and an ambiguous report with probability 0.5499, at expected cost 2.479. Full identification matches the target-safe reporting probabilities but has higher expected cost, 4.679, because it additionally measures the target-irrelevant attribute.

The benchmark therefore motivates the reportability layer rather than establishing novelty for target-oriented design.

\subsection{Additional dimensions}
Across the full parameter grid, primary outputs are correct deterministic reporting, wrong deterministic reporting, honest ambiguity, expected cost, and effort spent on target-irrelevant distinctions. Target-switch analysis confirms that relevance is defined by the declared target, while false-resolution sensitivity shows the expected transition between abstention and deterministic reporting as the admissible error threshold changes.

\paragraph{Results 1--2: the reporting infrastructure.}
The operational findings above depend on two exact quotient results. They are reported here as formal infrastructure rather than as stand-alone novelty claims.

\subsection{Result 1: experiment-induced quotient and honest reporting}
For design $D$, let $\operatorname{record}_D(w)$ denote the complete record generated under world $w$.

\begin{definition}[Experiment-induced quotient]
For a design $D$,
\[
w\sim_D w' \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \operatorname{record}_D(w)=\operatorname{record}_D(w').
\]
The quotient $W/{\sim_D}$ is the experiment-induced ecological quotient.
\end{definition}

An observed record $x$ induces the compatible class
\[
C_D(x)=\{w\in W:x\text{ is allowed under }w\text{ and }D\}.
\]
The exact prediction report is $T(C_D(x))=\{T(w):w\in C_D(x)\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Experiment information and honest target report]
Every report computed only from the experiment record is constant on experiment-induced classes. A deterministic record-based report of $T$ is justified exactly when $T$ is constant on the relevant compatible class. Otherwise the sharp exact report is the corresponding set of compatible target values.
\end{theorem}

A record can therefore identify a present state while leaving several target values compatible. A sharper point prediction would add information the experiment did not supply.

\subsection{Result 2: minimal resolution of future-relevant distinctions}
\begin{theorem}[Unique coarsest target-safe quotient]
For a declared finite evidence problem, there exists a unique coarsest equivalence relation that preserves current observations, preserves target reports under every declared future action, and is stable under action successors. Every other observation-preserving, action-stable quotient supporting the same target conclusions refines it.
\end{theorem}

The result is asymmetric: full latent-world identification is unnecessary when remaining differences imply the same future, but a small distinction must be retained when it can change the target. The construction is a target-relative application of established partition-refinement logic rather than a claim that state minimization itself is new \cite{GivanDeanGreig2003}.

\subsection{Ecological scope}
The same reportability structure applies when degraded ecosystems have competing recovery mechanisms, invasive populations have uncertain eradication response, declining populations share an observed trend but differ in demographic limitation, or alternative-state models agree on current regime membership but disagree about reversibility. The relevant latent distinction is the one that changes the declared future, not a taxon-specific state descriptor.

\section{Discussion}
The central distinction is finite reportability, not target orientation by itself. Forecasting and goal-oriented design already focus on downstream predictions; Paper B instead represents a realized finite record by the target values that remain compatible, then asks which latent distinctions and observation guarantees are required before that report can become a defensible singleton.

This changes the role of model complexity. Full mechanism identification is unnecessary when remaining distinctions cannot alter the declared future, whereas a subtle difference must remain explicit when it reverses recovery, persistence, transition, or intervention response. The target-safe quotient makes that requirement executable while preserving set-valued output when the evidence is insufficient.

Observation architecture is part of the same reporting logic. With exact failure probabilities, shared failures can impose a realized resolution ceiling; with availability specified only by lower bounds, they instead impose a ceiling on the guarantee that can be certified uniformly from the declared contract. Additional within-mode replication cannot substitute for genuinely distinct failure opportunities when a strong guarantee is required.

The framework remains conditional on the declared world set, actions, observation models, targets, costs, and finite support. It does not infer missing mechanisms or failure factors, replace management utility, or provide a universal continuous-state convergence theorem. Its value is auditability: every singleton conclusion can be traced to the target-relevant worlds excluded by the evidence, and every retained target set to a future distinction that remains unresolved.

Finite evidence should therefore be judged by what it can report about a declared future, not only by how much uncertainty an experiment removes. Target-safe monitoring retains only the distinctions needed for the future, checks whether the observation architecture can support them, and reports a singleton only within an explicit false-resolution contract. Otherwise, the honest ecological prediction remains a set.
