\subsection{Sensitivity analyses and practical scope}
\subsubsection{Boundary with goal-oriented design and value of information}
The detailed literature boundary is established in the Introduction. In brief, target-oriented experimental design is not unique to this framework: targeted and goal-oriented OED optimize predictions or quantities of interest \cite{VanlierEtAl2012,AttiaEtAl2018,ZhongEtAl2026}, and ecological VOI values information through management consequences \cite{CanessaEtAl2015}. Table~\ref{tab:method-boundaries} therefore locates target-safe modelling as a finite reportability layer rather than as a replacement for those objectives.

\subsubsection{Finite representation and posterior-sample bridge}
The exact theorems are conditional on a declared finite world set; no universal continuous-state convergence theorem is claimed. Posterior draws, particles, scenarios, or ensemble members can supply a finite support, but refinement of that support may reveal target distinctions absent from a coarser approximation. The deterministic invasion-control bridge demonstrates this interface rather than empirical validation: across 500, 2,000, and 10,000 posterior draws, the response assay remains selected for the eradication-versus-containment target while source tracing does not resolve that target (Table~\ref{tab:posterior-bridge}).

\subsubsection{Example of a changed ecological action}
For a detected invasive population, current occurrence, eradication success, and source pathway are different targets. The first may require no further response experiment, the second can require an intervention-response assay, and the third can make provenance measurement decisive. The framework therefore changes which observation is reportability-relevant rather than merely recommending more monitoring, consistent with objective-driven conservation monitoring \cite{NicholsWilliams2006}.
