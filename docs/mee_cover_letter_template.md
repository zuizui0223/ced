Dear Editors of *Methods in Ecology and Evolution*,

Please consider our manuscript, **“From ecological states to distinguishable futures: Target-safe prediction from finite evidence,”** for publication as a Standard Research Article in *Methods in Ecology and Evolution*.

Ecological monitoring methods often quantify uncertainty about latent states or optimize new observations for a specified prediction. Our manuscript addresses a complementary methodological problem: **what a realized finite record is sufficient to report about a declared ecological future**. We formalize this as a finite reportability interface. A record induces a compatible-world structure and a sharp target set; a unique coarsest action-stable quotient retains only the distinctions needed for the declared future; observation-failure architecture determines whether the required distinctions can be trusted; and an explicit false-resolution contract governs adaptive stopping and singleton reporting.

We position the method conservatively against its nearest neighbours. We do not claim novelty for imperfect-detection modelling, set-valued prediction, state abstraction, goal-oriented experimental design, value of information, or question-driven monitoring in isolation. The contribution is their integration into an auditable ecological reporting object that distinguishes **what an experiment aims to learn** from **what the realized evidence can defensibly support**.

The paper combines exact finite results with executable software and deterministic validation. The four headline results are accompanied by a formal Supplement, exhaustive finite checks where appropriate, an exact 16-world benchmark, failure-architecture comparisons at equal nominal effort, target and error-contract sensitivity analyses, and a posterior-sample bridge showing how the finite interface can operate on support drawn from a continuous Bayesian model. All manuscript numbers, tables, and figures are regenerated in continuous integration across Python 3.10–3.12.

We believe the work fits *Methods in Ecology and Evolution* because the method is not tied to one taxon or data type: the same reportability problem occurs whenever current ecological evidence leaves target-relevant mechanism or response distinctions unresolved. The implementation is designed as an auditable layer that can sit downstream of occupancy, demographic, ecosystem, ensemble, or Bayesian models rather than replacing them.
